Prepare the fish fillets (the key for tender fish)
Cut the boneless fish into 0.5cm thick fillets, put them in a bowl.
Add 1 tbsp cooking wine, a pinch of salt, a little white pepper, mix well and marinate for 5 minutes to remove fishy smell.
Add 1 egg white and 2 tbsp cornstarch, stir evenly until each fillet is coated with a thin paste (this locks in moisture and keeps fish tender).
2. Preprocess the pickled mustard greens and side dishes
Rinse the pickled mustard greens with water (reduce sourness if too salty), squeeze out excess water, and cut into small pieces.
Blanch enoki mushrooms/bean sprouts in boiling water for 10 seconds, drain, and spread on the bottom of a deep bowl (serves as the base).
3. Sauté the aromatics and pickled mustard greens
Heat 2 tbsp cooking oil in a pot over medium heat, add ginger slices, scallion whites and minced garlic, stir-fry until fragrant.
Add the cut pickled mustard greens, stir-fry for 2 minutes to release the sour aroma (do not over-fry to avoid losing crispness).
4. Simmer the soup base
Pour hot bone broth/boiling water into the pot, add 1 tsp light soy sauce (adjust salt according to the saltiness of pickled mustard greens), bring to a gentle boil and simmer for 3 minutes to blend the flavors.
5. Cook the fish fillets (low heat to avoid breaking)
Turn the heat to low (the soup should be slightly bubbling, not rolling boil), slide the marinated fish fillets into the pot one by one, do not stir immediately.
After 1 minute, gently push the fillets with a spatula, cook for another 1-2 minutes until the fish turns white and floats (overcooking will make it tough).
Pour the entire pot of fish and soup into the deep bowl with blanched side dishes at the bottom.
6. Finish with spicy and numbing oil (the soul of the dish)
Sprinkle dried chili peppers and Sichuan peppercorns on top of the fish fillets, and add chopped scallion greens.
Heat the remaining 1 tbsp cooking oil in a small pot until it smokes slightly (about 70-80°C), pour it directly over the chili peppers and Sichuan peppercorns—you’ll hear a sizzling sound, which releases the spicy and numbing aroma instantly.
